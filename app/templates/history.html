The World's First Bakers

Although many people believe breadmaking began in Egypt or Mesopotamia 17,000 years ago, archaeological evidence has proven that Indigenous Australians were the first to make bread. In 1991 a grind stone from cuddie springs (right) containing starch remnants was dated back 30,000 years. More recently, in 2015 a similar stone found in Arnhem Land is believed to have been used 65,000 years ago.

Agriculture

Indigenous Australians weren't just hunters and gatherers, they developed complex dam irrigation systems, food preservation boxes made of materials like clay and straw, and sustainable aquacultural techniques. A key feature of Indigenous agriculture was a commitment to caring for the environment. This involved only taking what was necessary, aiding plant regeneration through cool burns, and rotating agricultural areas to prevent overuse.

First Nation's Food Today

Today, although landscapes and typical diets have changed drastically due to the introduction of foreign animals like sheep, there's still a strong legacy of Indigenous Australian food culture. Particularly in recent years there's been a growing interest in local, native produce, resulting in a push to expand the commercial availability of native produce. However, it's also important to acknowledge the history of these ingredients and allow Indigenous communities control over the commercialisation of them in order to avoid the unethical dispersion of traditional produce.
